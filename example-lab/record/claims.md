# Claims — LIVE

**What may be said, at what strength.** Every claim cites the findings that license it.

## C-1 · On nearly-sorted input, insertion sort needs fewer comparisons than merge sort only while disorder stays around one percent

**Licensed by:** F-1, F-2
**Strength:** systematic, one size — 20 seeds per disorder level at 1000 elements.
**Bounds that travel with it:** comparisons, not time; 1000 elements; disorder by random swaps.
**Must not be said:** "insertion sort is faster on nearly-sorted data" — time was never measured.

## Gap list — what we must NOT claim, and why

- "Insertion sort wins for small arrays" — the small-array experiment found the opposite on comparisons (its row is in the ledger), and time was not measured.
